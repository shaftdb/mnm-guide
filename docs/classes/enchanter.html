Enchanter

Enchanters are subtle spellcasters who focus their study on magic which affects the mind and manipulates the ephemeral. Above all else, the Enchanter specializes in control and subterfuge. An Enchanter’s spellbook is diverse and situational. It is here, in those unique situations that the application of their magics is most powerful. Their greatest strength in battle is their ability to assault the minds of their foes causing them harm, incapacitating them momentarily, or turning them against their allies. Enchanters are also capable of imbuing their allies' weapons with magical effects, causing them to inflict greater harm with their blows. An Enchanter is most at home when adventuring with others, and their role within adventuring parties is primarily that of one who controls the battlefield. What each stat does (wiki) fact
  • STA: Increases health and reduces chance to be dazed in combat (wiki Statistics; '1 STA = 1 HP' is marked 'need testing' there).
  • AGI: Provides damage mitigation (wiki Statistics).
  • STR: Increases melee and ranged weapon damage and shield block amounts; 1 STR = 2 weight (wiki Statistics).
  • DEX: Increases hit rate, weapon proc frequency and gives a channeling bonus to spellcasters (wiki Statistics).
  • INT: Mana pool size if INT is higher than WIS, and offensive spell damage (wiki Statistics).
  • WIS: Mana pool size if WIS is higher than INT, and healing done; 4 mana per WIS point for WIS-based classes (wiki Statistics).
  • CHA: Merchant prices and charm break rate on paper; the wiki notes CHA did not seem to affect vendor prices in beta (May 2026).

Traits

fact You pick one Major Combat, one Minor Combat, one Major Non-Combat and one Minor Non-Combat trait; your race also grants a racial ability (wiki Traits).

Major Combat: Intelligent strategy

Your Intelligence is increased by 5%. Active: Become intelligent, increasing your Intelligence by 50% for 15 seconds.

Intelligent: +5% INT and a 15-second +50% INT burst. Charismatic if you lean on charm.

Alternatives: Charismatic, Tough

Minor Combat: Alterer strategy

Your Alteration skill starts at 10. Your Alteration skill increases 25% faster. ''Alteration spells heal and enhance allies, and hinder enemies.''

Alteration is the biggest Enchanter school on the wiki (95 of 136 spells). Channeler keeps mesmerizes landing when you are hit.

Alternatives: Channeler, Abjurer

Major Non-Combat: Readiness strategy

You start with an available Cantrip slot, which can be assigned to a memorized spell to retain it after death.

Readiness gives a Cantrip slot from level 1, so one memorized spell survives death.

Alternatives: Forage, Infravision, Ultravision

Minor Non-Combat: Miner strategy

Your Mining skill starts at 25. Your Mining skill increases 5% faster. You start with a Trainee's Mining Pick and Trainee's Mining Pouch.

Miner starts Mining at 25 with a Trainee's Mining Pick and Pouch, matching the default gathering setup (Mining, Lumberjacking, Smelting) for early coin. Medic (Bind Wound 25 and bandages) is the safer pick if you expect downtime between fights. Blacksmith exists but smithing stays off by default here.

Alternatives: Medic, Lumberjack

fact Official class page — defining traits:

  • Premier crowd-control class
  • Relies on Charisma as a primary stat, directly affecting the strength of their spells
  • Controls encounters through incapacitates, stuns, and charms, enabling CC‑driven strategies that can manage the most chaotic situation
  • Has a suite of powerful buffs, including mana regeneration, melee hastes, and damage absorption
  • Has debuffs that reduce enemy resistances and slow their attacks
  • Uses illusions to alter appearances for social, factional, and situational utility
What the devs say is changing

The Enchanter’s existing toolkit is solid and their identity is clear, so the update focuses on scaling and depth over addition and removal. Charm is being tuned; it's currently stronger than intended and will be brought into better balance, but it remains a core part of how the class plays. Illusions are getting more practical value, expanding their usefulness in social and factional situations.

More: Scaling and upgrades in core ability lines; Practical value from illusions

Less: Generic utility spells that don't reflect the Enchanter's focus on mental influence and control
