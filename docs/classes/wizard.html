Wizard

Wizards are dedicated scholars of the magical arts. A Wizard seeks to become a master of magic through study and practice. As the forerunners of magical knowledge and resource, they hold the ability to cast the most complex and dangerous spells available to mortals. Wizards excel at bringing battles to a swift end from afar with correctly chosen and deliberately timed spells. Should they become too depleted to use their spells, they are able to enter melee, delivering magically enhanced blows that reinvigorate themselves. Wizards are also capable of making foes more vulnerable to magic, enhancing allies' blows with extra effects, and manipulating the fabric of reality with teleportation focused magic. Bonus-point advice (wiki)

Below is the recommended allocation of bonus points by race. Since Intelligence is a Wizard’s main attribute, you should focus on maximizing it whenever possible. Your spell damage and mana total is directly tied to it. After that, prioritize Dexterity. It affects hit rate across the board and increases your proc chance. Finally, put some points into Stamina for extra HP and a bit of stun resistance. Dwarf: +1 Stamina, +2 Dexterity, and +7 Intelligence Gnome: +3 Stamina, +2 Dexterity, and +5 Intelligence Human: +1 Stamina, +2 Dexterity, and +7 Intelligence

  • fact Do the Ail'Vorith Starter Quest from the spawn point: quarry rock, then Supplier Jul, the grinding wheel, the newbie-yard hunt, Cook Vashuk and the Provost. Source: Wiki: Ail'Vorith Starter Quest
    Ail'Vorith Starter Quest Recommended lvl 1

    strategy Required: the Obsidian Shackle it gives unlocks every House class quest, and through them city access.

    GiverToilmaster Verith or Toilmistress Rallvessa
    WhereA few feet from where new Deep Elves spawn, outside the Foreign Quarter footbridge
    Level1
    Classesany
    Needs firstNew Deep Elf character started in Ail'Vorith (you spawn holding the Imbued Parchment). Set chat language to Deep Common.
    FactionNo faction change listed on the wiki pages: no sourced data yet.
    Steps fact
    1. Read the Imbued Parchment and hand it to Toilmaster Verith or Toilmistress Rallvessa.
    2. Use /look crate and /take pickaxe on the crate labelled REPAIRED to get a Slave Pickaxe (the BROKEN crate's pickaxe fails and Verith slaps you for 2 damage). Hand it in; it is given back.
    3. Go south into the water and follow the wall right to a small quarry cave. Equip the pickaxe and mine 20 Fragile Quarry Rock (nodes respawn in 5-10 seconds).
    4. Hand in the 20 rock and the pickaxe for a Pack of Stone (you become encumbered). Carry it across the footbridge to Supplier Jul, the first building on the left in the Foreign Quarter.
    5. Agree to make sharpening stones ('I will do it'). You get a Sharpening Stone Satchel and Recipe: Fragile Sharpening Stone (right-click to learn). Take 5-8 Fragile Quarry Rock from the crate next to Jul (/take rock).
    6. Through the market gate, right through the tunnel, right fork, first building on the right: use the grinding wheel by Blacksmith Zorvek to make 5 Fragile Sharpening Stones. Put them in the satchel and combine.
    7. Return the Full Sharpening Stone Satchel to Jul for the Sharpening Stone Delivery Satchel, then give it to Verith. Answer that you made them, then that you are prepared, to receive a Small Leather Hunting Satchel.
    8. Hunt the newbie yard for 2 each: Bone Chips (a decayed skeleton), Deep Beetle Legs (a small beetle, a deep beetle), Damaged Fungus Cap (a cave sporeling). Put them in the satchel in single stacks and combine.
    9. Hand the Filled Leather Hunting Satchel to Verith for the Satchel of Questionable Spoils. Deliver it to Cook Vashuk (inn past the courtyard fountain, back-right doorway, downstairs, kitchen).
    10. Hail Verith, say Yes to rejoining the Houses, and take Verith's Recommendation to Provost Xan'shorn (walks in circles on the left of the courtyard).
    11. He clasps the Obsidian Shackle on your wrist. Enter the Obsidian Academy and find your House representative (see the House quest for your class).

Their core identity stays intact. They’ve still got the biggest nukes, the most raw magical damage, and the high ceiling that rewards players who put in the work.

Design notes (official deep dive)

Welcome to our second class deep dive! We’re making great progress on the first four classes in our update queue, so keep an eye out for more of these posts in the near future.

Today we’ll be looking at the Wizard. We’ve already completed the work and are testing their new kit internally. We’re trying to iron out any bugs and oddities before bringing it to the beta servers, but even then, it’s inevitable that we’ll learn something new when it gets into the hands of thousands of players. Expect a bit of iteration as time goes on.

Our plan is to complete a solid first pass on all 18 classes, which involves getting the right abilities in place and making sure each class has its intended toolkit. While this phase does involve some tuning, we’ll certainly need to revisit each class afterward to ensure everything works well and they feel relatively balanced.

Once a set of changes reaches beta, expect further tweaks and tuning if anything turns out to be majorly wrong. There should be a quick turnaround if something’s outright broken, but otherwise, we'll move on to the next class in line with the goal of bringing all 18 to beta as quickly as we reasonably can.

Now, onto the Wizard.

There are a couple of items we mentioned in the initial Wizard class update post that have shifted somewhat.

Abilities

While Wizards can definitely teleport allies, we didn’t add any new summoned items in this round. Wizards still summon a short-duration light source, but that’s all for now. Once we finish more updates, we’d like to revisit summoned items across the board, particularly when it comes to the Elementalist.

This was an existing Wizard ability that we got a lot of feedback on—most of it negative. A common concern was that it felt too random or difficult to get a killing blow, particularly in group situations. Additionally, since the amount of mana returned was tied to damage done, crits and resists felt especially bad.

  • “Receives a portion of mana spent when delivering a killing blow with a spell”
  • “Out of combat, Wizards can research and customize spells”

Damage Spells

Wizards are masters of magic, so it's only right that they get a tool for every situation. Many of their spells have a secondary effect in addition to dealing damage; for example, ice spells snare the target briefly, lightning spells interrupt casting, and arcane spells decrease the target’s casting speed. We kept a good mix of magic, fire, cold, and electric spells, since every Wizard needs a Fireball.

When designing the spell lines, we tried to give each a strong identity, with tradeoffs between damage, cast time, and mana efficiency. These are likely to see continued adjustments in the future as we get more class updates done, so we can ensure that Wizards are the major damage dealers they should be.
