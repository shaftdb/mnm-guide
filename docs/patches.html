Wizard

  • Volley spells are now much more sustainable as a primary source of damage.
  • Multiple wizards can now play more easily together, as they have their own individual stacks for Reclaim Magic using the new Spark of Magic system described below.
  • Seal of Magic
  • While Seal of Magic is active, your Evocation spells still activate Mark of Magic, and your harmful spells now also leave Sparks of Magic on your target.
  • Mark of Magic
  • Mark of Magic works as before and is shared by everyone. It increases all Spell Damage the target takes by 2% per stack, up to five stacks, for 1 minute. Reclaim Magic no longer consumes it, so the damage bonus stays up while your group keeps casting.
  • Spark of Magic
  • Spark of Magic is new and belongs to the Wizard who left it. Your spells leave more Sparks the more Mana they cost, and Volleys leave the most. Sparks last 1 minute, lower the target's Maximum Mana slightly, and can't be dispelled.
  • Reclaim Magic
  • Reclaim Magic now consumes your own Sparks and returns Mana based on how many you had. It no longer has a cooldown, and grants your party Arcane Acceleration if you don't already have it.
  • Reclaim Magic can't use another Wizard's Sparks, and another Wizard can't use yours. With more than one Wizard in the group, every Wizard can build and reclaim on the same target without taking stacks from each other.
  • Arcane Volley and Fire Volley
  • Arcane Volley and Fire Volley now hit five times at every rank, and their damage per hit is about the same as before. Reclaiming after a full Volley returns more Mana than it cost, so alternating Volley and Reclaim Magic lets you keep casting while slowly regaining Mana.

  • Sanctified Weapon grows stronger as the blessed ally gains levels. The two blessings don't stack with each other, so use whichever fits the moment.

Spell scroll prices

  • We've reworked how spell and ability scrolls are priced so that every class spends the same amount to learn its full kit.
  • The old prices were set per level, so a scroll cost about the same no matter who bought it. That sounds fair until you count the scrolls. A wizard has far more abilities to learn than a monk, so a wizard ended up paying roughly four times as much to buy everything. We couldn't find a good reason for that - A wizard isn't more powerful than a monk, doesn't need less gear, and doesn't earn more money along the way.
  • Now each 5-level range has a set total cost that's the same for every class. For levels 1-5, buying all of your scrolls costs 4sp 5cp, whether that's 6 scrolls or 24. If your class has lots of scrolls in a range, each one is cheaper. If it has only a few, each one costs more, but everyone spends the same total.
  • Within a range, earlier scrolls cost less than later ones, so your level 1 scrolls are cheaper than your level 5 scrolls.
  • A few scrolls are shared between classes, like Gate. Those have one price for everyone, set low enough to fit every class that uses them.
  • This does create some eye-catching prices at high level, where some melee classes only get one or two new abilities in a range. We have built the game with the expectation that all classes should be competitive and useful with their full kit, so pricing those to require equal spend feels like a sensible choice.

Blind Spells

  • We'll be reworking Blind spells soon after Early Access starts, and expect to remove the cooldown after we update the spell's effects.

Shaman

  • Each cast delivers the same total healing over a shorter duration, so the healing lands faster.
  • Consuming Pact: has been adjusted slightly at some levels to account for the earlier Pact of Renewal ranks. Mana returned and health cost are unchanged.

Lull Spells

  • Lull Beasts and Nature's Calm spell lines now have a 30 yard range, no additional cooldown, and can each lull one target at a time.

Class Changes

  • Buff Durations
  • Goal - We'd like to find a good balance between class interdependence, and social play for the buffs you can cast on others, while making the process of buffing and rebuffing a bit cheaper and faster.
  • During the beta period, many buffs were set to a one hour duration to make it easier to test and to get benefit from social buffing.
  • While we want players to buff outside of their group as a form of social cooperation, the hour-long buffs are reducing the appeal of bringing buffing classes into groups.
  • Here are the changes we made in this update:
  • We've reduced mana hour-long buff durations to be 35 minutes instead.
  • We've also adjusted mana costs to be cheaper in most cases.
  • Additionally, we've shortened the cast times on many of these buffs to be a bit quicker.
  • Most self-only and pet buffs were not reduced to a shorter duration as they do not affect class interaction goals.
  • We've added some group versions of common buffs in recent class updates, and will consider adding more in the upcoming class updates.
  • While we understand that this change will not please everyone, we are committed to making a game with strong social connection required for success and also providing a pace of play that includes some amount of intentional downtime that leads to more social play.
  • Buffs and rebuffing are a natural resting point, where mana-driven DPS classes can pause and mediate, the group can chat, and take a quick afk if needed.
  • Single-Target Healing Update
  • We’ve adjusted both the healing amounts and mana costs for most single‑target healing spells. Several factors led to this change:
  • Wisdom scaling overshot expectations. A previous update added a large bonus to healing based on Wisdom. This was a positive change that rewards you for gearing up, but our baseline tuning didn’t account for how large that bonus would become. As a result, heal amounts and mana costs drifted out of alignment.
  • Healing inflation emerged. Many players reported that heals were simply too large relative to actual player health, making incoming damage feel trivial.
  • Mana efficiency became too high. With the Wisdom bonus, most heals, especially heal‑over‑time and delayed heals, became more mana‑efficient than intended. This reduced the sense of danger and made mana management less meaningful.
  • HoTs and delayed heals were under‑costed. These spells should be somewhat more efficient because they don’t deliver full healing immediately, but they were providing too much value for their cost. While they have different mechanics than a normal heal, they are all valuable tools for healing in different situations, and didn't need a big mana efficiency bonus to be valuable.
  • Why this matters:
  • Healing and mana management are central to group pacing and social play. Healers should feel rewarded for managing their mana well, and groups should benefit from playing smart to reduce unnecessary healing. Occasional pauses for healer mana:
  • let casters catch up on regen,
  • create natural social moments,
  • and give players brief breaks without disrupting the group.
  • We’ve consistently aimed for a somewhat slower, more deliberate pace than modern MMOs - something that makes dungeon camping feel dangerous without becoming non-stop action with no time to chat or eat that sandwich.
  • What Changed
  • Healing amounts were reduced to more reasonable values.
  • Mana costs were adjusted to match the new healing numbers.
  • Single‑target heals were re‑evaluated for consistency and value.
  • In some cases, mana costs decreased while efficiency stayed the same or improved.
  • In others, especially HoTs and delayed heals, heal amounts and efficiency were reduced because they were previously over‑performing.
  • What Happens Next
  • As always, we’ll monitor player feedback and beta server data to see how these changes feel in real gameplay. We’ll continue refining healing and mana balance to support the pacing and group dynamics we’re targeting.

General

  • All pets can now innately dual wield starting at level 36
  • All pets now gain double attack starting at level 20

Balance

  • Many weapon items were previously using DPS ratios that did not properly reflect their rarity and the level of their sources, and have now been corrected to scale as expected
  • Several basic items still need to be tuned to ensure consistent DMG increases each tier (ex: rusty iron weapons)
  • Many armor items were previously using AC values that did not properly reflect their rarity and the level of their sources, and have now been corrected to scale as expected
  • The allocation of AC per slot has been shifted, moving more AC to slots like shoulders, head, and shield, with less AC on "accessory" slots like belts, capes and rings
  • AC totals for existing players may end up higher or lower depending on the quality of equipment you're using in each slot, but total AC in equivalent gear should be roughly the same in the long run (higher when using shields)
  • 2H weapon DPS ratios have been refactored  they will now scale more consistently compared to 1H counterparts
  • In practice, this means lower DMG values in the early game, but higher DMG values by the endgame
  • Class restrictions have been applied to shields based on the type of shield (ex: buckler, kite shield, tower shield, etc). The model will not always match the shield category—you can expect continued updates over time
  • Modify charm & fear logic in relation to Charisma
  • Reduce all stat contributions by 25% to allow for more headroom. Items are being modified to accommodate the new budgets
  • Strafe runspeed bonus has been removed
  • You must sit for more than one tick to start meditating
  • Equipment health regen now only considers the single largest health regen value (no longer adds them all)
  • You can no longer get dazed when being attacked from the front
  • Harvest Nodes now have respawn times and can no longer be cheesed

Combat

  • Unarmed damage has been rewritten for Monks

  • Player base health & Stamina scaling has been updated
  • Shadowknights, Paladins & Inquisitors will now scale at the same rate as Fighters
  • All other player classes have been refactored to new categories with higher base health & health per Stamina
  • Ranged physical damage now scales from Strength like other physical damage sources
