Tips

fact Official class page — defining traits:

  • Wears plate armor and a shield for tanking, or wields a two-handed weapon for DPS
  • Divine power is expressed through melee strikes and physical contact rather than spellcasting
  • Fast-casting emergency healer at a high mana cost, excelling at the last-second save rather than sustained output
  • Can absorb harm intended for allies, sacrificing their own resources or health in service of others
  • Can consecrate the ground with distinct properties, giving them terrain control that feels physical rather than arcane
  • Has a passive divine aura that provides ongoing benefits to nearby allies
  • Has a strong anti-undead toolkit expressed through combat and presence
  • Has a distinct tanking style built around divine resilience and protection
What the devs say is changing

The Paladin is gaining some distance from the Cleric. Both serve the divine protector fantasy, but they’re being separated at the mechanical level so they feel nothing alike in practice. Thematically, the Cleric works through prayer and incantation, whereas the Paladin works through physical action, touch, and presence. Abilities that blurred these lines are being removed or reconfigured.

The other shift is in healing. The Paladin is becoming more distinct by using fast, mana-intensive heals as last-second saves rather than providing sustained healing. The ability to take damage on behalf of their allies reinforces this theme.

Auras and consecrated ground are becoming the primary vehicle for party utility, replacing more generic buff and spell options.

More: Physical expression of divine power through strikes, touch, and presence; Auras and consecrated ground as primary party utility; Fast, mana-intensive emergency healing; Sacrifice mechanics that absorb harm on behalf of allies; Distinct tanking identity separate from other tank classes

Less: Abilities shared with or derived from the Cleric; Traditional spellcasting and incantation; Generic utility

Group: Tank with heals/consecrations; Kick back at L3 and Crusader Strike bonus threat (0.30.2.1).

strategy Duo partners: Cleric, Shaman, Druid, Shadow Knight — chosen to cover what Paladin lacks (Tank, Healer).

Stat points

fact Class modifier: +2 STR, +4 STA, +2 WIS (wiki Paladin Creation Guide). Every listed race gets 10 bonus points at creation (wiki race pages).

unverified Mana: The official Paladin page mentions mana costs, but no spell on the wiki list has a numeric mana cost, so which stat sets your mana is not sourced yet.

What each stat does (wiki) fact
  • STA: Increases health and reduces chance to be dazed in combat (wiki Statistics; '1 STA = 1 HP' is marked 'need testing' there).
  • AGI: Provides damage mitigation (wiki Statistics).
  • STR: Increases melee and ranged weapon damage and shield block amounts; 1 STR = 2 weight (wiki Statistics).
  • DEX: Increases hit rate, weapon proc frequency and gives a channeling bonus to spellcasters (wiki Statistics).
  • INT: Mana pool size if INT is higher than WIS, and offensive spell damage (wiki Statistics).
  • WIS: Mana pool size if WIS is higher than INT, and healing done; 4 mana per WIS point for WIS-based classes (wiki Statistics).
  • CHA: Merchant prices and charm break rate on paper; the wiki notes CHA did not seem to affect vendor prices in beta (May 2026).

Traits

fact You pick one Major Combat, one Minor Combat, one Major Non-Combat and one Minor Non-Combat trait; your race also grants a racial ability (wiki Traits).

Major Combat: Tough strategy

Your Stamina is increased by 5%. Active: Become tough, increasing your Stamina by 50% for 15 seconds.

Tough: +5% STA and a 15-second +50% STA emergency button.

Alternatives: Wise, Strong

Minor Combat: Shieldman strategy

Your Block skill starts at 5. Your Block skill increases 25% faster. You start with a Trainee's Buckler.

Shieldman: Block at 5 (+25%) and a Trainee's Buckler for shield tanking from level 1.

Alternatives: Defender, Channeler

Major Non-Combat: Forage strategy

Search your surroundings, attempting to gather food, drink, and other supplies.

Forage finds food and drink in the field, so fewer vendor runs. Paladins may take it. Infravision or Ultravision only if your race may take it (see the race note).

For a Halfling: Infravision (you already have Forage as a racial).

Alternatives: Infravision, Ultravision, Slow Metabolism

Minor Non-Combat: Miner strategy

Your Mining skill starts at 25. Your Mining skill increases 5% faster. You start with a Trainee's Mining Pick and Trainee's Mining Pouch.

Miner starts Mining at 25 with a Trainee's Mining Pick and Pouch, matching the default gathering setup (Mining, Lumberjacking, Smelting) for early coin. Medic (Bind Wound 25 and bandages) is the safer pick if you expect downtime between fights. Blacksmith exists but smithing stays off by default here.

Alternatives: Medic, Lumberjack
