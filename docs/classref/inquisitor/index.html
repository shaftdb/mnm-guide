Tips

fact Official class page — defining traits:

  • Plate-wearing melee tank with a focus on mental domination and anti-magic specialization
  • Can silence, dispel, mana drain, and produce magical barriers
  • Excels against spellcasters while remaining capable against physical opponents
  • Employs mind control tools including a brief charm, ability denial, and mental shutdown
  • Redistributes stolen mana to allies and can enhance their magical abilities
What the devs say is changing

The Inquisitor's identity is being separated from the Enchanter. Both classes use mind control and ability denial, but they’ll feel very different in practice. Inquisitor versions of these tools are being rebuilt around aggression and proximity rather than finesse.

The grab-bag utility is being cut and consolidated, freeing up ability slots for tools that actually fit the class. Against melee opponents, the Inquisitor now has options, but using them costs mana, which creates a real tradeoff against their primary role.

More: Fast casting and short-range abilities; Unique abilities focused on eroding an opponent’s willpower, making them more vulnerable to attack and damaging their psyche; Consolidation of existing abilities to take up less slots while being more effective; Differentiation in their tanking style; Tools to help against melee opponents at the cost of mana

Less: Shared abilities and identity with the Enchanter; Broad utility

Solo: Wiki: some difficulty alone.

Group: Protector / off-tank with caster disruption; Kick back at L3 (0.30.2.1).

strategy Duo partners: Cleric, Shaman, Druid — chosen to cover what Inquisitor lacks (Tank, CC).

Stat points

fact Class modifier: +1 STR, +3 STA, +1 DEX, +2 INT, +1 CHA (wiki Inquisitor Creation Guide). Every listed race gets 10 bonus points at creation (wiki race pages).

fact Mana: Your mana stat is whichever of INT or WIS is higher (wiki Statistics); 30 of 72 wiki Inquisitor spells cost mana. Deep Elf Inquisitor: INT 21, WIS 17, so your mana stat is INT.Human Inquisitor: INT 18, WIS 16, so your mana stat is INT.

What each stat does (wiki) fact
  • STA: Increases health and reduces chance to be dazed in combat (wiki Statistics; '1 STA = 1 HP' is marked 'need testing' there).
  • AGI: Provides damage mitigation (wiki Statistics).
  • STR: Increases melee and ranged weapon damage and shield block amounts; 1 STR = 2 weight (wiki Statistics).
  • DEX: Increases hit rate, weapon proc frequency and gives a channeling bonus to spellcasters (wiki Statistics).
  • INT: Mana pool size if INT is higher than WIS, and offensive spell damage (wiki Statistics).
  • WIS: Mana pool size if WIS is higher than INT, and healing done (wiki Statistics).
  • CHA: Merchant prices and charm break rate on paper; the wiki notes CHA did not seem to affect vendor prices in beta (May 2026).

Group tank (default) strategy

+4 STA · +3 AGI · +3 STR

The official page and wiki both describe the Inquisitor as a plate tank and 'protector'. STA is health and daze resistance, AGI is mitigation and STR adds weapon damage and shield block, so every point goes into surviving and holding threat with Censuring Strike / Distress damage.

Result: Deep Elf: STR 18, STA 22, DEX 17, AGI 21, INT 21, WIS 17, CHA 16Human: STR 20, STA 24, DEX 17, AGI 19, INT 18, WIS 16, CHA 18

Anti-caster / mana strategy

+4 STA · +4 INT · +2 AGI

Your starting INT is higher than WIS for both playable races, so INT is your mana stat (wiki Statistics). The official page says the reworked melee tools 'cost mana', so a bigger pool helps long fights against casters. Pick this if you mostly group with casters or tank caster-heavy camps.

Result: Deep Elf: STR 15, STA 22, DEX 17, AGI 20, INT 25, WIS 17, CHA 16Human: STR 17, STA 24, DEX 17, AGI 18, INT 22, WIS 16, CHA 18

Solo-leaning strategy

+4 STR · +3 STA · +3 DEX

The wiki class page says Inquisitors 'may find some difficulty' alone. STR and DEX raise weapon damage and hit rate, which is most of your solo kill speed early; STA keeps you alive. Expect to regroup for dungeons.

Result: Deep Elf: STR 19, STA 21, DEX 20, AGI 18, INT 21, WIS 17, CHA 16Human: STR 21, STA 23, DEX 20, AGI 16, INT 18, WIS 16, CHA 18

Traits

fact You pick one Major Combat, one Minor Combat, one Major Non-Combat and one Minor Non-Combat trait; your race also grants a racial ability (wiki Traits).

Major Combat: Tough strategy

Your Stamina is increased by 5%. Active: Become tough, increasing your Stamina by 50% for 15 seconds.

Tough: +5% STA and a 15-second +50% STA emergency button, the most direct tank survival pick. Magic Resistant fits the anti-mage identity (you are 'the specialist called in when the enemy wears robes'). Agile if you prefer mitigation.

Alternatives: Magic Resistant, Agile

Minor Combat: Shieldman strategy

Your Block skill starts at 5. Your Block skill increases 25% faster. You start with a Trainee's Buckler.

Shieldman starts Block at 5, raises it 25% faster and gives a Trainee's Buckler, which suits a sword-and-board tank from level 1. Defender (Defense skill) is the other tank pick. Channeler helps keep your fast casts going while you are being hit.

Alternatives: Defender, Channeler

Major Non-Combat: Forage strategy

Search your surroundings, attempting to gather food, drink, and other supplies.

Forage (allowed for Human and Deep Elf Inquisitors) finds food and drink, which cuts vendor runs. As a Deep Elf starting in Ail'Vorith you live in the Deep, so Ultravision ('greatly enhanced' darkness vision, worse in bright light) is the stronger quality-of-life pick there; Infravision is not available to Human or Deep Elf.

For a Deep Elf: Ultravision.

Alternatives: Ultravision (Deep Elf only), Slow Metabolism

Minor Non-Combat: Miner strategy

Your Mining skill starts at 25. Your Mining skill increases 5% faster. You start with a Trainee's Mining Pick and Trainee's Mining Pouch.

Miner starts Mining at 25 with a Trainee's Mining Pick and Pouch, matching this Codex's default gathering setup (Mining, Lumberjacking, Smelting) for money. Medic (Bind Wound 25, bandages) cuts a tank's downtime between pulls. Blacksmith exists but smithing stays off by default here.

Alternatives: Medic, Survivalist

Race pros and cons

Human strategy

fact Neutral · racial: Tenacity (as of Feb 16, 2026) · resists: Corruption -5, Holy -5 · starts (Inquisitor): Night Harbor

Pros

  • Tenacity (racial): +15% resistance to Mesmerize and Incapacitate, and an active 15-second immunity. Strong for a tank that faces casters.
  • Highest starting STA of the playable Inquisitor races (20 vs 18) and CHA 18.
  • Neutral alignment; starts in Night Harbor, the hub with the most documented quests on the wiki.

Cons

  • Corruption -5 and Holy -5 resistance.
  • Lower INT (18) than a Deep Elf, so a smaller mana pool from the same points.
  • Can only start in Night Harbor as an Inquisitor (no Underdocks option).

Deep Elf strategy

Pros

  • INT 21 and AGI 18 at creation: more mana and mitigation from the start.
  • Ultravision available as a trait.
  • Only Inquisitor race that can start outside Night Harbor (Ail'Vorith), with its own Inquisitor trainer and the House Xal'tharn class quest.

Cons

  • Lower STA (18) and STR (15) than a Human.
  • Racial ability not documented on the wiki ('unknown').
  • Evil alignment: Night Harbor is described as the start for good and neutral combinations; how Night Harbor treats Deep Elves is not sourced yet.

Tradeskills strategy

  • Mining: Pairs with the Miner trait; the Ail'Vorith starter quest also has you mine Fragile Quarry Rock with a Slave Pickaxe.
  • Smelting: Turns ore into sellable bars (separate skill on the wiki).
  • Lumberjacking: Second gathering skill for coin, part of this Codex's default set.
  • Bind Wound: Bandaging between pulls; useful for a tank's downtime.

Gathering defaults plus Bind Wound. Blacksmithing (smithing) stays off by default; switch it on in the Trades menu if you want to craft plate later. STRATEGY.

Starting near Night Harbor

fact Underdocks is the only start city right next to Night Harbor, but no race can start there as an Inquisitor. The only non-Night-Harbor Inquisitor start is a Deep Elf in Ail'Vorith, which is far away in the Deep.

  • Underdocks fact: Adjacent zone to Night Harbor (wiki zone infobox); the official FAQ calls it 'a seedy underground port district found far below Night Harbor'. Not an Inquisitor start for any race or deity (wiki start-city table).
  • Ail'Vorith fact: Under the eastern lands of Calafrey (official FAQ). From Underdocks by boat through the Caves of Irem and Great Cavern Sea (wiki Ail'Vorith and Underdocks pages). Deep Elf Inquisitor only, for deities Agnostic, Bulibyre, Erythar, Maelsin Thrys, Trimminax, Urzebol and Ygorath (wiki start-city table). Has an Inquisitor trainer (Nurvol Cereveth) and the House Xal'tharn class quest.
  • Faelindral fact: In Evershade Weald, in Calafrey to the north (official FAQ, wiki). Wood Elf and Halfling only; no Inquisitor.

Best Inquisitor start outside Night Harbor strategy Deep Elf Inquisitor in Ail'Vorith is the only way to start an Inquisitor outside Night Harbor, and it has its own class trainer and class quest. If you want to stay close to Night Harbor instead, play a Human (Night Harbor start) and head down to the Underdocks, one zone away, once you have finished the Night Harbor starter quest.
