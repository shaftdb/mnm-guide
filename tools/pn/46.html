0.30.0.0 - Early Access Patch + Module 1

September 19, 2026

  • Adjusted stealth detection:
  • Added a front cone proximity detection range bonus for players detecting stealthed players/npcs.
  • Mesmerization buff overwriting is now determined by spell duration rather than caster level. A longer lasting Mesmerize will always overwrite a lower duration one.
  • Hide is now based on Stealth rather than invisibility. As such, it will work against Undead, but will have an increasingly larger detection distance from the front of NPCs that con red.
  • Most group buffs are now available to purchase from spell vendors directly, rather than as research-granted spells.
  • We do plan to add additional spells and abilities from research in the future.

Charmed NPC damage has been reduced from its previous level. While charmed NPCs are weaker, they are still a valuable source of extra damage in a group situation and when soloing if you choose to use it. Certainly with weaker charmed pets, soloing will not be as efficient and fast as it was previously, but it is still a viable tactic to increase your damage.

This affects the Charm spells of Enchanters, Elementalists, Druids, Bards, and Necromancers. For classes that have a pet already, the charmed pet damage is an upgrade of at least 25% more than your summoned pet. For classes without a summonable pet, the damage is lower as it's purely a bonus and does not require you to give up your summoned pet in order to use it. Inquisitors are not affected by this change, as their Charm ability has both a very long cooldown and a short duration which felt like enough of a limitation to balance the power of their ability.

This is a topic we've received lots of feedback on during Beta, and not everyone will agree with this change. In the past, charmed pet damage often exceeded the amount of damage you could get by adding 2-3 more DPS classes to a group, and was unquestionably the highest damage spell in the game by a wide margin. This change will be good for game balance, and allow us to tune difficulty to be more reasonable for diverse groups.
